# Movie Nights artwork sources

## Active artwork policy — 2026-09-21

The historical notes below describe retained source files, not the current rendering policy. The Metahub inventory in `../manifest.json` records 300 × 450 posters and a 1280 × 720 Pulp Fiction backdrop. Neither the older Wikipedia notes nor the Metahub provenance documents a redistribution license for high-resolution studio artwork. No replacement images were scraped, downloaded without a license, or upscaled.

Phase 9.2.6 explicitly permits deterministic fallback when licensed high-resolution artwork is unavailable. All bundled film and series records therefore use original genre illustrations generated by `movies/movies.js`. These are scalable vectors, with a 2:3 poster crop and a landscape hero composition. They are not theatrical posters, official stills, or photographs. The historical raster files remain unchanged for provenance and compatibility with saved records; they are not selected by current seed data. Manifest dimensions continue to report their real sizes, not the requested sizes.

The renderer supports future licensed poster pairs through `src`, `width`, `height`, `smallSrc`, and `smallWidth`, serving `srcset` and `sizes`. Poster masters must be at least 1000 × 1500; landscape masters at least 1920 × 1080. CSS and image-load handling cap raster display width at its intrinsic width. Correct 2:3 posters fill their frame with `object-fit: cover`; arbitrary uploaded images may be cropped. The generated fallback remains sharp at every viewport size.

This is the recorded licensing exception for steps 9.2.1–9.2.3 and the artwork clause of 9.3.2; no claim is made that licensed photographic replacements were delivered.

**Resolution note (2026-09-14, revised):** every file above is only 220-265px wide. This isn't an accident of the download — it's Wikipedia's non-free-content policy working as designed: fair-use images of copyrighted posters are deliberately capped at low resolution so they can't substitute for the commercial artwork. Wikipedia (and by extension Wikimedia Commons, which only hosts freely-licensed content and has no legitimate high-resolution stills for these actively-copyrighted films either) can never provide a larger version of these specific images, for any film still under copyright. That makes these files fine for their current use — small library/card poster thumbnails, where 220-265px is native resolution, not upscaled — but unsuitable as full-bleed hero backdrops, which is why the hero background no longer uses them at all (see below).

The hero spotlight background is now a generated per-movie gradient scene (color derived from each movie's `accent` field in `movies-data.js`, rendered in `movies.js`'s `renderSpotlight()`), not a photograph. This is deliberate, not a placeholder-until-better-art-arrives: genuine high-resolution (1920x1080+) official backdrop stills for mainstream studio films are not available under a license that permits this kind of reuse through any free source we could find (Wikipedia's cap above; Wikimedia Commons searches for these titles turned up only unrelated real-world photos, unverified fan art, and misattributed matches). A generated scene is also infinitely sharp at any hero size, which a raster image capped at 220px never can be. Every movie in the catalogue — the 5 listed above and all others — gets the same generated-scene treatment for its hero; only the small library/card poster thumbnails ever use these fetched images.
